What the chart shows

  1. The console’s age matters more than any single game. Almost every line climbs for three to five years and then falls, whatever is released. Switch to “Years since launch” and the arcs line up. Late hits like The Last of Us (PS3, 2013) and Ghost of Tsushima (PS4, 2020) landed on consoles already in decline.
  2. The best-selling consoles had hits early and kept them coming. Wii Sports, Wii Fit and Mario Kart Wii arrived in the Wii’s first 18 months, and the Wii peaked at 24.8M in 2008. Breath of the Wild, Mario Kart 8 Deluxe and Odyssey all came in the Switch’s first year, and its sales grew every year through 2020.
  3. Big games did not rescue weak consoles. Super Mario 3D World, Mario Kart 8 and Splatoon were Wii U highlights, yet it never passed 3.4M a year. Uncharted: Golden Abyss didn’t lift the Vita either.
  4. Misses didn’t sink healthy consoles. Xbox One sales rose in 2015 and 2016, the years of Halo 5 and Quantum Break, and PS5 kept selling about 20M a year when Concord flopped in 2024.
  5. Other factors often explain the spikes. The Switch’s 27.4M peak in 2020 came with Animal Crossing and pandemic lockdowns at the same time. PS5 and Series X|S were short of stock until 2022, so their early years show supply, not demand.

Verdict: the data fits exclusives as a steady pipeline that builds a console early, not as one-off sales spikes. No single game visibly changes a console’s path, but every console that sold well had several strong exclusives in its first two years. Yearly totals can show the pattern; they can’t prove cause.

About the data

Calendar years, worldwide units. 2026 covers January to September only (hollow square end points), so its drop is partly the short year.

Company-reported (solid lines): Wii, Wii U, DS and GameCube from Nintendo’s quarterly shipments; Switch from Nintendo’s figures (2017–23 compiled by analyst Paul Gale); Switch 2 from Nintendo’s figures; PS4 and PS5 from Sony’s quarterly sell-in, regrouped from fiscal quarters into calendar years.

Estimates (dashed lines): Xbox 360, Xbox One, Series X|S, PS3, PSP, PS Vita and 3DS come from VGChartz yearly estimates, because Microsoft and Sony don’t publish yearly figures for them. Their totals land within a few million of official lifetime figures (e.g. PS3 87.4M, 3DS 75.9M).

Not shown: PS2, original Xbox, Game Boy Advance and GameCube before 2005, because there is no consistent yearly data for them. Game numbers in the tooltips are lifetime figures from the Console Wars poster; misses show no number.

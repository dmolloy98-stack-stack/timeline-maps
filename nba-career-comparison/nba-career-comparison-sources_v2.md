# Jordan vs. Kobe vs. LeBron: source log (v2)

Map: `nba-career-comparison_v2` (calendar-year view and career-year view). Data as of Nov 19, 2026; fact-checked Nov 20, 2026.

Every award count was checked in at least two independent sources. Every date on the map was checked in at least two opened pages unless it is marked **single source** below. Every link in this log was opened during the fact-check and showed the cited content, except where a note says the page left a detail out. Pages that came back empty are not listed.

Season-by-season details that were checked:

- **Jordan:** no games in 1993–94 (baseball), 1998–99, 1999–00 or 2000–01. 1994–95 was 17 games. Bulls 1984–93 and 1994–98; Wizards 2001–03. Title years 1991, 92, 93, 96, 97, 98. MVP years 1988, 91, 92, 96, 98.
- **Kobe:** Lakers for all 20 seasons, 1996–97 to 2015–16. Title years 2000, 01, 02, 09, 10. MVP 2008.
- **LeBron:** Cleveland 2003–10 and 2014–18, Miami 2010–14, Lakers 2018–26, 76ers 2026–27. Title years 2012, 13, 16, 20. MVP years 2009, 10, 12, 13.
- **Titles by decade:** computed from the title years above (Jordan 6 in the 1990s; Kobe 4 in the 2000s and 1 in the 2010s; LeBron 3 in the 2010s and 1 in the 2020s).

## Conventions

- The calendar view labels each season by its start year (1984 = the 1984–85 season). Off-season events (retirements, signings, trades) sit at the start of the season that follows.
- In the career-year view, Year 1 is each player's first NBA season. Jordan's non-playing years are hatched and still count as career years.
- MVP means regular-season MVP. Title years in the data table are the year the Finals ended; the season column shows the season label.
- All-Star counts run through 2025–26.
- Original design. No logos, photos or artwork.
